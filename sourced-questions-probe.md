## bus-pedestrian (small-claims, plaintiff, 45.2s; written 5, refused by code 0, by the check 0)

- **On what date did the bus hit you?**
  - Why: The Limitations Act, 2002 says a proceeding generally cannot be commenced after the second anniversary of the day the claim was discovered.
  - Limitations Act, 2002, S.O. 2002, c. 24, Sched. B, s. 4: "after the second anniversary of the day on which the claim was discovered"
- **Have you applied for statutory accident benefits?**
  - Why: The Insurance Act requires a person to apply for statutory accident benefits before commencing an action for bodily injury arising from the use or operation of an automobile.
  - Insurance Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. I.8, s. 258.3 (1): "(a) the plaintiff has applied for statutory accident benefits;"
- **Have you served written notice of your intention to sue on anyone you intend to sue, and if so, when?**
  - Why: The Insurance Act requires written notice of the intention to sue within 120 days after the incident, unless the court authorizes a longer period.
  - Insurance Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. I.8, s. 258.3 (1): "the plaintiff served written notice of the intention to commence the action on the defendant within 120 days after the incident"
- **Have you notified an insurer that you intend to apply for accident benefits, and if so, when?**
  - Why: The Statutory Accident Benefits Schedule requires notice to the insurer no later than the seventh day after the circumstances giving rise to benefits, or as soon as practicable after that day.
  - Statutory Accident Benefits Schedule, O. Reg. 34/10, s. 32 (1)-(3): "shall notify the insurer of his or her intention no later than the seventh day after the circumstances arose that give rise to the entitlement to the benefit, or as soon as practicable after that day."
- **Have you provided any information about your injury claim to anyone you intend to sue, and if so, when?**
  - Why: The Insurance Act requires the plaintiff to provide the defendant with the information prescribed by regulations within the time period prescribed by regulations before commencing the action.
  - Insurance Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. I.8, s. 258.3 (1): "(c) the plaintiff provided the defendant with the information prescribed by the regulations within the time period prescribed by the regulations;"

## charter-search (civil, plaintiff, 45.8s; written 5, refused by code 0, by the check 1)

> Two police officers came into my apartment last spring without a warrant and searched my bedroom. They said they were looking for someone else. Nothing was found and no charges were laid. I want to sue the police service for violating my rights and for the stress it caused.

- **Did the officers say they were looking for the other person to arrest or apprehend them?**
  - Why: The Criminal Code permits warrantless entry under specified conditions for the purpose of arresting or apprehending a person.
  - Criminal Code, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-46, s. 529.3 (1)-(2): "for the purpose of arresting or apprehending a person"
- **What did the officers say or do that explained why they believed the person they were looking for was in your apartment?**
  - Why: The Criminal Code requires reasonable grounds to believe the person is present in the dwelling-house for this power of warrantless entry.
  - Criminal Code, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-46, s. 529.3 (1)-(2): "reasonable grounds to believe that the person is present in the dwelling-house"
- **What, if anything, did the officers say about why they could not wait to obtain a warrant before entering your apartment?**
  - Why: The Criminal Code requires urgent circumstances that make obtaining a warrant impracticable for this power of warrantless entry.
  - Criminal Code, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-46, s. 529.3 (1)-(2): "by reason of exigent circumstances it would be impracticable to obtain a warrant"
- **What, if anything, did the officers tell you about their authority to arrest the person they were looking for?**
  - Why: The Criminal Code requires an arrest warrant in force or grounds to arrest or apprehend without a warrant as a condition for an entry warrant.
  - Criminal Code, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-46, s. 529.1: "a warrant referred to in this or any other Act of Parliament to arrest or apprehend the person is in force anywhere in Canada"

## human-rights-employment (civil, plaintiff, 49.2s; written 5, refused by code 0, by the check 0)

> I was fired from my warehouse job three weeks after I told my manager I was pregnant. They said it was restructuring but they hired someone for my position a month later. I want to sue them for wrongful dismissal and discrimination.

- **On what date did you first learn you were being fired?**
  - Why: The Limitations Act, 2002 bars starting a proceeding after the second anniversary of the day the claim was discovered, unless the Act provides otherwise.
  - Limitations Act, 2002, S.O. 2002, c. 24, Sched. B, s. 4: "after the second anniversary of the day on which the claim was discovered"
- **How long had you worked for the employer when your employment ended?**
  - Why: The Employment Standards Act, 2000 sets different termination notice periods depending on the employee's length of employment.
  - Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41, s. 57: "at least one week before the termination, if the employee's period of employment is less than one year"
- **How much notice, if any, did the employer give you before your employment ended?**
  - Why: The Employment Standards Act, 2000 requires termination notice to be given before termination, with the required period depending on length of employment.
  - Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41, s. 57: "The notice of termination under section 54 shall be given"
- **What efforts have you made since being fired to find work with similar pay and responsibilities?**
  - Why: Brake v. PJ-M2R Restaurant Inc. says a wrongfully dismissed employee must make reasonable best efforts to obtain work reasonably comparable in salary and responsibility.
  - Brake v. PJ-M2R Restaurant Inc., 2017 ONCA 402, paras. 157-158: "making reasonable best efforts to obtain a position that is reasonably comparable in salary and responsibility"
- **How, if at all, has the alleged discrimination affected your dignity, feelings or self-respect?**
  - Why: The Human Rights Code allows a court to award compensation for loss arising from a rights infringement, including injury to dignity, feelings and self-respect.
  - Human Rights Code, R.S.O. 1990, c. H.19, s. 46.1 (1)-(2): "including compensation for injury to dignity, feelings and self-respect"

  - Why: The Children's Law Reform Act allows an objection by notifying the person proposing the move or making an application, no later than 30 days after receiving notice.
  - Children's Law Reform Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.12, s. 39.3 (4)-(7): "no later than 30 days after receiving the notice"
- **Does your parenting order provide for roughly equal time with each parent, and how closely have you both followed that schedule?**
  - Why: The Children's Law Reform Act puts the burden of proving the move is in the child's best interests on the relocating parent when both parties substantially follow an equal-time order.
  - Children's Law Reform Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.12, s. 39.4 (3)-(5): "If the parties to the proceeding substantially comply with an order, family arbitration award or agreement that provides that a child spend substantially equal time in the care of each party"

## mechanic-holding-car (small-claims, 38.6s; written 5, refused by code 0, by the check 0)

> The garage said it would be around $600 for brakes and now wants $1,400 and won't give my car back until I pay.

- **What work or repairs did you authorize the garage to do?**
  - Why: The Consumer Protection Act, 2002 says a repairer cannot charge for work or repairs unless the consumer authorized them.
  - Consumer Protection Act, 2002, S.O. 2002, c. 30, Sched. A, s. 58 (1)-(2): "No repairer shall charge for any work or repairs unless the consumer authorizes the work or repairs."
- **Did the garage give you the estimate in writing?**
  - Why: The General regulation under the Consumer Protection Act, 2002 requires an estimate of the total cost of vehicle work and repairs to be in writing.
  - General regulation under the Consumer Protection Act, 2002, O. Reg. 17/05, s. 48: "an estimate of the total cost of work on and repairs to a vehicle shall be in writing"
- **What amount, if any, did you agree to pay the garage for the repairs?**
  - Why: The Repair and Storage Liens Act says a repairer's lien can be for the amount the person requesting the repair agreed to pay.
  - Repair and Storage Liens Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. R.25, s. 3 (1)-(2): "The amount that the person who requested the repair agreed to pay."
- **Has the garage completed all the repairs, or only part of them?**
  - Why: The Repair and Storage Liens Act says that where only part of a repair is completed, the lien amount is the fair value of the part completed.
  - Repair and Storage Liens Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. R.25, s. 3 (1)-(2): "Where only part of a repair is completed, the fair value of the part completed"
- **What is the value of the car the garage is holding?**
  - Why: The Courts of Justice Act limits Small Claims Court claims for recovery of personal property to property whose value does not exceed the prescribed amount.
  - Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43, s. 23 (1)-(2): "where the value of the property does not exceed the prescribed amount."

## both-at-fault-fence (small-claims, 41.6s; written 5, refused by code 0, by the check 0)

> A guy backed into my fence with his truck. He says I'm partly to blame because my fence was over the property line a bit.

- **On what date did the truck hit your fence?**
  - Why: The Limitations Act, 2002 presumes a claim was discovered on the day the act or omission took place, unless the contrary is proved.
  - Limitations Act, 2002, S.O. 2002, c. 24, Sched. B, s. 5 (1)-(3): "unless the contrary is proved"
- **Have you filed a plaintiff's claim with the Small Claims Court, and if so, when?**
  - Why: The Limitations Act, 2002 generally bars starting a proceeding after the second anniversary of the day the claim was discovered.
  - Limitations Act, 2002, S.O. 2002, c. 24, Sched. B, s. 4: "a proceeding shall not be commenced in respect of a claim after the second anniversary of the day on which the claim was discovered"
- **Was the truck on a road or somewhere else when it hit your fence?**
  - Why: The Highway Traffic Act places the burden of disproving negligence on the vehicle's owner, driver, lessee or operator when damage arises from a motor vehicle on a highway.
  - Highway Traffic Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. H.8, s. 193 (1)-(4): "When loss or damage is sustained by any person by reason of a motor vehicle on a highway"
- **Where was the fence in relation to the property line and the area where the truck was backing up?**
  - Why: The Negligence Act requires damages to be divided according to each party's degree of fault when the plaintiff's fault contributed to the damage.
  - Negligence Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. N.1, s. 3: "if fault or negligence is found on the part of the plaintiff that contributed to the damages"

  - Southcott Estates Inc. v. Toronto Catholic District School Board, 2012 SCC 51, paras. 23-24: "As a general rule, a plaintiff will not be able to recover for those losses which he could have avoided by taking reasonable steps."

## parenting-decisions (family, 34.1s; written 5, refused by code 0, by the check 0)

> We can't agree on who the kids should live with after the separation. What does the court look at?

- **What needs does each child have for physical safety, emotional well-being and stability?**
  - Why: The Children's Law Reform Act gives primary consideration to the child’s physical, emotional and psychological safety, security and well-being.
  - Children's Law Reform Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.12, s. 24 (1)-(3): "shall give primary consideration to the child’s physical, emotional and psychological safety, security and well-being."
- **How has each parent been involved in caring for the children before and since the separation?**
  - Why: The Children's Law Reform Act says the court considers the history of care of the child.
  - Children's Law Reform Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.12, s. 24 (1)-(3): "(d) the history of care of the child;"
- **What plans does each parent have for the children's day-to-day care?**
  - Why: The Children's Law Reform Act says the court considers any plans for the child’s care.
  - Children's Law Reform Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.12, s. 24 (1)-(3): "(g) any plans for the child’s care;"
- **What have the children expressed about where they want to live, and how old are they?**
  - Why: The Children's Law Reform Act says the court considers the child’s views and preferences, giving due weight to age and maturity, unless those views cannot be ascertained.
  - Children's Law Reform Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.12, s. 24 (1)-(3): "(e) the child’s views and preferences, giving due weight to the child’s age and maturity, unless they cannot be ascertained;"
- **What has each parent done to support the children's relationship with the other parent?**
  - Why: The Children's Law Reform Act says the court considers each parent’s willingness to support the child’s relationship with the other parent.
  - Children's Law Reform Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.12, s. 24 (1)-(3): "(c) each parent’s willingness to support the development and maintenance of the child’s relationship with the other parent;"
