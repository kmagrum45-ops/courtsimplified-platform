 What if I&#8217;m not hired because an employer discriminates against me?

 Next steps

 1. Find out if Ontario&#8217;s laws apply

 2. Learn when human rights laws might not apply

 3. Check if you can make a human rights claim 

 4. Learn more about making a human rights claim

 There are steps you can take if an employer discriminates against you in a way that goes against Ontario's human rights laws. You might be able to make a claim against the employer with the  Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario .
 If the  Tribunal decides that the employer discriminated against you, it could order them to:

 pay you money
 take human rights training
 develop human rights policies

 What the law says about discrimination
 Employers cannot discriminate for reasons that go against human rights laws.
 This usually means that an employer cannot refuse to hire you  because of a personal characteristic that's listed in the Human Rights Code. These personal characteristics relate to your:

 race, colour, ancestry, place of origin, and ethnic origin
 citizenship
 religion
 sex, gender, gender identity , and gender expression
 sexual orientation
 family and marital status
 disability
 age

 And an employer cannot discriminate against you because you've been convicted of a crime , if you have a record suspension . A record suspension used to be called a pardon.
 Read more in Can an employer ask me if I have a police record?
 Employers’ rules
 Some employers say they have rules that apply to everyone. And they only hire people who can follow these rules. This could be  discrimination  if you cannot follow a rule for a reason that goes against your human rights.
 For example, an employer might have a dress code or rule about what people must wear at work. They say that the rule applies to everyone. But the employer may have to let you dress differently if you wear something for religious reasons.
